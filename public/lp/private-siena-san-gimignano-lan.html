A private day trip to Siena, Monteriggioni and San Gimignano — from Florence, or from wherever you are staying in Italy

Three of Tuscany's medieval masterpieces in one day, with a private car and driver at your disposal from your front door in Florence and back again. This is the same journey the coaches make — and nothing like the same day.

Siena

A scenic hour through the Tuscan countryside brings you to Siena, the city built of the brown bricks that gave the colour its name. Narrow streets and sudden squares, medieval towers making a skyline you will recognise from a hundred photographs, and one of the most important Gothic cathedrals south of the Alps.

The shell-shaped Piazza del Campo is the heart of it: a brick-paved slope where, twice a year, the Palio horserace turns the whole city inside out. Above it stands the tall, trim Torre del Mangia. You have two and a half hours here and no group to keep up with, so you can climb the tower, sit with a coffee, or simply walk. If you would like a licensed private guide in Siena to open the history for you, we can arrange two hours at €250 for your party — ask when you book, it cannot be added on the day.

The Tuscan countryside and Monteriggioni

Between Siena and San Gimignano the road runs through the landscape people come to Italy for: vineyards, olive groves, rolling hills, cypresses, old stone farmhouses. Because the car is yours, when someone in the back says stop, look at that, the car stops.

Halfway you pause at Monteriggioni, a tiny walled village raised in the 13th century as a Sienese fortress against Florence, its ring of towers still complete. Dante saw it and put it in the Inferno. The shared coaches drive past it; on a private tour you get out and walk in.

San Gimignano

The afternoon belongs to San Gimignano, the hilltop town famous for a skyline of thirteen surviving medieval towers, rising above the Elsa valley along the ancient Via Francigena that carried pilgrims and traders to Rome. UNESCO protects the whole centre.

See the Collegiate Church and its frescoes, the Palazzo del Popolo, the Museum of Torture if that is your family's sense of humour. Wander the main street in and out of the artisan shops — ceramics, leather, linen — taste the crisp white Vernaccia di San Gimignano in one of the wine bars, and eat what is regularly called the best gelato in Italy. Then, when you are ready and not a minute before, the drive home to Florence.
